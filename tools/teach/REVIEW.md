## New grammar cards

### Unit 2: Three endings: -u, -a, -i ✓
- Arabic: الْمَسْجِدُ كَبِيرٌ · أَرى الْمَسْجِدَ · فِي الْمَسْجِدِ
- English: The mosque is big · I see the mosque · in the mosque
- Explanation: Most Arabic words can end in -u, -a or -i, and the ending shows the word’s job. -u is the usual one: what the sentence is about. -a is for the thing something is done to. -i comes after words like فِي (in), مِنْ (from) and إِلى (to), and on the second word of ‘the X of Y’. Without ال, add an extra -n: -un, -an, -in. Later cards point back to these three endings.
- Crossed-out mistake: ~~فِي الْمَسْجِدُ~~, After فِي the word ends in -i: فِي الْمَسْجِدِ.

### Unit 2: The past: the ending shows who ✓
- Arabic: ذَهَبْتُ · ذَهَبْتَ · ذَهَبْتِ · ذَهَبَ · ذَهَبَتْ · ذَهَبْنا
- English: I went · you went (to a man) · you went (to a woman) · he went · she went · we went
- Explanation: In the present, the first letter shows who. In the past, the ending does: ـتُ for ‘I’, ـتَ for ‘you’ (a man), ـتِ for ‘you’ (a woman), nothing extra for ‘he’, ـتْ for ‘she’ and ـنا for ‘we’. To change a sentence to the past, take the verb’s past form and put the right ending on it.
- Crossed-out mistake: ~~أَنا ذَهَبَ إِلى الْمَسْجِدِ.~~, With أَنا the past verb ends in ـتُ: أَنا ذَهَبْتُ إِلى الْمَسْجِدِ.

### Unit 3: Describing words copy the noun ✓
- Arabic: بَيْتٌ كَبِيرٌ · غُرْفَةٌ كَبِيرَةٌ · الْبَيْتُ الْكَبِيرُ
- English: a big house · a big room · the big house
- Explanation: A describing word comes after the noun and copies it: feminine if the noun is feminine (add ة), ال if the noun has ال, and the same ending. The gender rule holds in a sentence too: الْغُرْفَةُ كَبِيرَةٌ, ‘the room is big’.
- Crossed-out mistake: ~~الشَّقَّةُ جَمِيلٌ.~~, شَقَّة (flat) is feminine, so: الشَّقَّةُ جَمِيلَةٌ.

### Unit 3: ‘This is a house’ or ‘this house’? ✓
- Arabic: هَذا بَيْتٌ · هَذا الْبَيْتُ
- English: This is a house · this house
- Explanation: Without ال, هَذا بَيْتٌ is a whole sentence: ‘This is a house.’ With ال, هَذا الْبَيْتُ is just ‘this house’, and the sentence goes on: هَذا الْبَيْتُ كَبِيرٌ, ‘This house is big.’
- Crossed-out mistake: ~~هَذا الْبَيْتُ.~~, To say ‘This is a house’, leave off ال: هَذا بَيْتٌ.

**‘The’ and ‘a’**

- *ال means ‘the’*: بَيْتٌ (a house) · الْبَيْتُ (the house) — Put ال on the front of a word for ‘the’. There’s no word for ‘a’: the -un ending (ٌ) does that job. A word has one or the other, never both.
- *Sun letters: the ل goes quiet*: الْكِتابُ (al-kitāb: the book) · الشَّمْسُ (ash-shams: the sun) — Before half the letters (like ك, ب, م, ق) you say al-. Before the other half, the sun letters (like ش, س, ت, د, ر, ن), the ل is silent and the next letter doubles.

**Masculine and feminine**

- *Look for ة*: مُدَرِّسٌ (a teacher (a man)) · مُدَرِّسَةٌ (a teacher (a woman)) · صَدِيقٌ (a friend (a man)) · صَدِيقَةٌ (a friend (a woman)) — Every Arabic noun, a person or a thing, is masculine or feminine. Most feminine nouns end in ة, and adding ة to a man’s word often makes the woman’s: مُدَرِّس → مُدَرِّسَة. Things too: بَيْتٌ (a house) is masculine, غُرْفَةٌ (a room) is feminine.
- *A few are feminine without ة*: أُمٌّ (a mother) · أُخْتٌ (a sister) · بِنْتٌ (a girl) — These mean a woman, so they’re feminine even without ة.

### Reading rules 2: the hamza

**The hamza ء: a catch in the throat**: ء is a stop in the throat, like the catch in "uh-oh". It often sits on a seat: on ا (أ, or إ for an "i"), on و (ؤ) or on ي without its dots (ئ). The seat isn’t said: you hear only the catch and its vowel.

| Example | Read | Meaning | Said from | OK? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| أَنا | ʾanā | I |  | |
| سُؤال | suʾāl | a question |  | |
| ماء | māʾ | water |  | |

Check: What do you hear for the ؤ in سُؤال? → **a catch in the throat** (also offered: a "w", nothing at all). The و is only a seat: you hear the catch, "suʾāl".

**Madda: آ**: آ is an ا with a wavy line on top, the madda. It says a catch and then a long "ā".

| Example | Read | Meaning | Said from | OK? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| آمِين | ʾāmīn | amen |  | |
| الْقُرْآن | al-qurʾān | the Qurʾān |  | |

Check: How is آمِين said? → **āmīn** (also offered: amīn, imīn). آ is a long "ā", with a catch before it.

**ى at the end: a long "ā"**: At the end of a word, ى looks like ي without its dots, but it says a long "ā", like ا. It’s called alif maqṣūra.

| Example | Read | Meaning | Said from | OK? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| عَلى | ʿalā | on |  | |
| إِلى | ilā | to |  | |
| مُصْطَفى | muṣṭafā | Mustafa (a name) |  | |

Check: How is عَلى said? → **ʿalā** (also offered: ʿalī, ʿalay). ى at the end says "ā": عَلى is "ʿalā".

### Reading rules 3: the spellings in al-Fātiḥah’s first line

**The small alif ـٰ**: A small upright alif above a letter (ـٰ) is a long "ā" that isn’t written as a full letter. You’ll find it in هٰذا, and in الرَّحْمٰن in the first line of al-Fātiḥah.

| Example | Read | Meaning | Said from | OK? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| هٰذا | hādhā | this | هَذا | |
| الرَّحْمٰنِ | ar-raḥmāni | the Most Gracious | الرَّحْمانِ | |
| ذٰلِكَ | dhālika | that | ذَلِكَ | |

Check: How is الرَّحْمٰنِ said? → **ar-raḥmāni** (also offered: ar-raḥmani, ar-raḥmini). The small alif is a long "ā": raḥ-mā-ni.

**The joining alif**: The ا at the start of اسْم (name) and ابْن (son), and the ا of ال, has no hamza. At the start of a sentence you say it: "ism". After another word it’s silent, and you go straight on: بِسْمِ اللهِ is "bismillāh", not "bi-ismi Allāh".

### The basics: Describing words

**Describing words come after, and match**: A describing word comes after the noun and matches it: ة if the noun is feminine, and ال if the noun has ال. لُغَة (language) is feminine, so it’s الْعَرَبِيَّة.

**Grammar cards**

| Card | Example | Meaning | What it says | Quick check (right answer first) | OK? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ‘Will’: سَـ on the front | سَأَصُومُ غَدًا. | I’ll fast tomorrow. | Put سَـ on the front of a present verb and it means ‘will’: أَصُومُ, I fast; سَأَصُومُ, I’ll fast. سَوْفَ before the verb means the same: سَوْفَ أَصُومُ. | How do you say ‘we’ll break the fast’? → سَنُفْطِرُ / نُفْطِرُ / أَفْطَرْنا | |
| ‘Before’ and ‘after’ | قَبْلَ الْفَجْرِ · بَعْدَ الْمَغْرِبِ | before dawn · after sunset | قَبْلَ is ‘before’ and بَعْدَ is ‘after’. The word that follows ends in -i: قَبْلَ الْفَجْرِ, بَعْدَ الْإِفْطارِ. | Which fits? ‘after the prayer’: بَعْدَ ___ → الصَّلاةِ / الصَّلاةُ / الصَّلاةَ | |
| ‘I fasted’: the long vowel drops | أَصُومُ · صُمْتُ | I fast · I fasted | Some verbs have a long vowel in the middle: صامَ, he fasted; زارَ, he visited. With ‘I’, ‘you’ and ‘we’ in the past, the long vowel drops out: صُمْتُ, I fasted; زُرْتُ, I visited; صُمْنا, we fasted. | How do you say ‘I visited my grandmother’? → زُرْتُ جَدَّتِي / أَزُورُ جَدَّتِي / زارَ جَدَّتِي | |

**Grammar cards**

| Card | Example | Meaning | What it says | Quick check (right answer first) | OK? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ‘It hurts me’: endings on verbs | رَأْسِي يُؤْلِمُنِي. | My head hurts (me). | The endings you know from ‘my’ and ‘your’ also go on verbs, for ‘me’, ‘you’, ‘him’ and ‘her’: يُؤْلِمُنِي, it hurts me; يُؤْلِمُكَ, it hurts you; يُؤْلِمُهُ, it hurts him. On a verb, ‘me’ is ـنِي, not ـِي. | How do you say ‘the doctor helps me’? → يُساعِدُنِي الطَّبِيبُ / يُساعِدِي الطَّبِيبُ / أُساعِدُ الطَّبِيبَ | |
| Parts of the body that come in pairs are feminine | عَيْنِي تُؤْلِمُنِي · رَأْسِي يُؤْلِمُنِي | My eye hurts · My head hurts | Eye, ear, hand, leg and foot: parts that come in pairs are feminine, though they have no ة. So the verb starts with تُـ: عَيْنِي تُؤْلِمُنِي. Head, back and belly are masculine: رَأْسِي يُؤْلِمُنِي. | Which fits? يَدِي ___ → تُؤْلِمُنِي / يُؤْلِمُنِي | |
| ‘Must’: يَجِبُ أَنْ | يَجِبُ أَنْ تَأْخُذَ الدَّواءَ. | You must take the medicine. | يَجِبُ أَنْ means ‘must’. The verb after أَنْ ends in -a, not -u: تَأْخُذَ. It’s the same after لِـ, ‘in order to’. | Which fits? يَجِبُ أَنْ ___ كَثِيرًا. → تَنامَ / تَنامُ / نِمْتَ | |
| ‘Do’ and ‘don’t’ | خُذِ الدَّواءَ · لا تَذْهَبْ إِلى الْعَمَلِ | Take the medicine · Don’t go to work | Commands are short forms of the verb. Learn these four: خُذْ, take; اِشْرَبْ, drink; نَمْ, sleep; اِجْلِسْ, sit. To a woman they add ـِي: خُذِي. For ‘don’t’, put لا before the ‘you’ verb and drop its last vowel: لا تَذْهَبْ, don’t go. | How do you say ‘don’t eat’ (to a man)? → لا تَأْكُلْ / لا أَكَلْتَ / لَيْسَ تَأْكُلُ | |

**Grammar cards**

| Card | Example | Meaning | What it says | Quick check (right answer first) | OK? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ‘Faster than’: أَسْرَعُ مِنْ | الْقِطارُ أَسْرَعُ مِنَ الْحافِلَةِ. | The train is faster than the bus. | To compare, a describing word is rebuilt with أَ at the front: سَرِيع → أَسْرَع, faster; قَرِيب → أَقْرَب, nearer; رَخِيص → أَرْخَص, cheaper. ‘Than’ is مِنْ. It’s the same for masculine and feminine, and it never takes the extra -n. | How do you say ‘nearer than’? → أَقْرَبُ مِنْ / قَرِيبٌ مِنْ / أَقْرَبُ إِلى | |
| ‘The nearest’ goes before the noun | أَيْنَ أَقْرَبُ مَحَطَّةٍ؟ | Where is the nearest station? | Put the same word before a noun and it means ‘the most’: أَقْرَبُ مَحَطَّةٍ, the nearest station; أَفْضَلُ فُنْدُقٍ, the best hotel. The noun has no ال and ends in -in. | How do you say ‘the cheapest hotel’? → أَرْخَصُ فُنْدُقٍ / فُنْدُقٌ رَخِيصٌ / الْفُنْدُقُ الرَّخِيصُ | |
| ‘Which’ and ‘who’: الَّذِي and الَّتِي | الْفُنْدُقُ الَّذِي بِجانِبِ الْمَحَطَّةِ · الْحافِلَةُ الَّتِي تَذْهَبُ إِلى الْمَطارِ | the hotel which is next to the station · the bus which goes to the airport | الَّذِي joins more about a masculine word onto it; الَّتِي does the same for a feminine one. They’re used after a word with ال. | Which fits? الطَّائِرَةُ ___ تُغادِرُ الْآنَ → الَّتِي / الَّذِي | |
| ‘Won’t’: لَنْ | لَنْ أُسافِرَ غَدًا. | I won’t travel tomorrow. | لَنْ before a present verb means ‘will not’. Like أَنْ, it makes the verb end in -a: لَنْ أُسافِرَ. Don’t add سَـ. | How do you say ‘I won’t go’? → لَنْ أَذْهَبَ / لَنْ سَأَذْهَبُ / لَنْ ذَهَبْتُ | |

**Grammar cards**

| Card | Example | Meaning | What it says | Quick check (right answer first) | OK? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Telling what happened: the verb first | خَرَجَ الْأَسَدُ، ثُمَّ نامَ تَحْتَ شَجَرَةٍ. | The lion went out, then slept under a tree. | A story is told in the past, and the verb usually comes first: خَرَجَ الْأَسَدُ, ‘went out the lion’. For a feminine doer the verb ends in ـَتْ: خَرَجَتِ الْبِنْتُ. Join one event to the next with ثُمَّ, ‘then’. | How do you say ‘the girl laughed’? → ضَحِكَتِ الْبِنْتُ / ضَحِكَ الْبِنْتُ / ضَحِكْتُ الْبِنْتُ | |
| ‘Didn’t’: لَمْ | لَمْ يَأْكُلِ الْأَسَدُ الْفَأْرَ. | The lion didn’t eat the mouse. | For ‘didn’t’, put لَمْ before the present verb and drop its last vowel: يَأْكُلُ → لَمْ يَأْكُلْ, he didn’t eat. It looks present, but it means the past. You can also say ما with a past verb: ما أَكَلَ. | How do you say ‘I didn’t go’? → لَمْ أَذْهَبْ / لَمْ ذَهَبْتُ / لَنْ أَذْهَبَ | |

| كانَ الثَّعْلَبُ سَعِيدٌ. | كانَ الثَّعْلَبُ سَعِيدًا. | After كانَ the describing word ends in -an. | |
| لَمْ يَأْكُلُ الْأَسَدُ الْفَأْرَ. | لَمْ يَأْكُلِ الْأَسَدُ الْفَأْرَ. | After لَمْ the verb loses its -u (here a helping -i, before ال). | |
