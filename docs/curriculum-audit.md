# Rafiq curriculum audit (22 Oct 2026)

**Ask (owner):** go through Rafiq's content and analyse what each module teaches (the words, the grammar and so on). Explain what a reasonable student who uses the app daily would know, and when, and their level of Arabic. Look for ways to improve the content so it takes someone from no Arabic to a suitable level in "these two units". Add anything else useful, and be realistic.

I've read "these two units" as the two starter units, **Reading Arabic** (unit 00) and **The basics** (unit 0b). They're the ones built for someone with no Arabic, and they come before unit 1. The whole course is covered too.

This is an analysis only: no app content or data was changed. The data fixes in section 6 are for Samir, who owns the drills, the audio and the data files.

---

## 1. The short version

- **What a daily learner gets:**
  - The path is **136 steps, about 12½ hours of listed lesson time**.
  - At the default 10 minutes a day (2 steps), a learner who never misses a day finishes it in **about 68 days**. In practice, counting reviews, retries and missed days, it takes **about 3 months**.
  - At the end they've met **about 450 path words**, with ~220 more practice items available (verbs, joining words, essentials, body). They can read any **vowelled** Arabic slowly, understand **their salah word by word**, and introduce themselves, their family, their home and their day in short, simple sentences.
- **Their level:** solid **A1** in reading and grammar knowledge, reaching into A2 grammar. **A1 at best** in speaking and listening, and **pre-A1 for unvowelled text**.
- **Why that's realistic:** 12–25 hours is a small fraction of the ~90–100 guided hours it takes to reach A1 in a European language. Arabic is in the US Foreign Service Institute's hardest group: about 88 weeks of full-time study to professional level, against ~24–30 weeks for French or Spanish. So Rafiq is a strong **foundation and salah course**, not a route to conversation. That's fine, as long as the marketing says so.
- **The starter units are good but too thin:**
  - The reading starter skips spellings that appear in the very first line of al-Fātiḥah: the dagger alif in الرَّحْمٰنِ and the joining alif in بِسْمِ / اللهِ.
  - The letters are never reviewed once the starter is done.
  - The basics squeeze about ten grammar ideas into 36 minutes. Then unit 02 adds seven more grammar cards, including the past tense, the present tense and the case endings.
- **Gaps:**
  - **Missing on the path:**
    - Survival phrases: "I don't understand", "hello", "what does … mean?".
    - Core verbs: *know, understand, take, need, ask, answer, return, forget*.
    - Grammar: the future (سَـ / سَوْفَ), past and future negation (لَمْ / لَنْ), and "who / which" (الَّذِي).
  - **Meanwhile, slots go to rare words:** "College of Aviation", "Ruqʿa script", "stamp collecting", "tray of eggs", "water heater".
  - **Data errors:**
    - كُلْيَة "kidney" is in the Study unit, almost certainly instead of كُلِّيَّة "college".
    - ساعة is taught 4 times in unit 04.
- **Who actually uses it:** about 85% of sign-ups never come back for a second day, and the most active learner had studied on 5 days (19 Oct). So almost everyone who ever uses Rafiq only sees **the reading starter, the basics and unit 01**. Those are where improvements pay off first.

## 2. How the course is built

| Part | What it is | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Reading Arabic** (unit 00) | 7 letter groups by shape (all 28 letters, each with its joined forms and 3 example words), the vowel marks, 3 reading rules, a listening test (18 sound-alike words; 9/12 to pass) | 10 steps, ~51 min |
| **The basics** (unit 0b) | 6 short grammar lessons plus a check. Goal sentence: "As-salāmu ʿalaykum, my name is …, and I'm a student of Arabic." | 7 steps, ~36 min, 35 words |
| **Units 01–12** | Themed units. Each one: meet 10 words → hear the conversation → how it works → more words → practise → have the conversation (the AI judge marks your replies) → say it yourself (typed, judged) → unit test (pass to open the next unit) | 9–11 steps each, ~50–60 min, 22–45 words |
| **Practise area** (always open) | Verbs: 21 verbs in past, present and future, sound to Form X. Joining words: 9 groups. Essentials: numbers, ordinals, days, both calendars' months, colours, time. Body explorer: 48 words. Spelling bee. Drills. Weak spots. Mistakes | ~220 items |
| **Real-life scenes** (Complete) | Airport, doctor, masjid, ordering a meal, taxi, visiting a family | 6 × 10 lines |
| **Your salah** | Takbīr to taslīm word by word (11 parts) plus 10 short surahs, al-Husary's recitation | 224 different words |
| **Review** | FSRS spaced repetition at 90% target retention, plus recaps every 2 lessons. Only words met on the path count: practice-area items and the alphabet never come back in review | — |

**Daily goal:** set at sign-up from the minutes chosen.

| Minutes chosen | Goal | Path finished (no missed days) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 1 step | 136 days |
| 10 (the default) | 2 steps | 68 days |
| 20 | 4 steps | 34 days |

Review sessions also count towards the goal, so in practice new lessons go slower than this.

**Where it comes from:** the 12 themes, and much of the vocabulary, follow the classic Saudi textbook order of *Al-ʿArabiyyah Bayna Yadayk* Book 1. That's why the setting has riyals, thobes and university faculties. The dialogues are Rafiq's own (`src: "rafiq"`). Section 8 has the licence note.

## 3. What each module teaches

"Can do" is what a learner who finished the unit and kept up review can do. The levels are CEFR-style, and the guide is honest about each one.

| Unit | Words (main themes) | Grammar ("How it works" cards) | Can do afterwards | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **00 Reading Arabic** | — (example words only) | Letters and joined forms; fatḥa, kasra, ḍamma, sukūn, shadda, long vowels, tanwīn; ة, ال in a sentence, stopping | Sound out any **fully vowelled** word slowly; tell apart the hard sound pairs (س/ص, ت/ط, د/ض, ذ/ز/ظ, ح/ه/خ, ك/ق) | pre-A1 (decoding) |
| **0b The basics** | 35: house, book, student, teacher, name, language, mother, girl, room… | ال and sun letters; my/your/his/her endings, ة → ت; I/you/he/she, no word for "is"; masculine/feminine, هَذا/هَذِهِ; iḍāfa ("of"), adjectives after the noun and matching it; broken plurals; we/you (pl)/they | Say "My name is…, I'm a student", "This is a big house", "the teacher's book" | A1 (start) |
| **01 Greetings** | 40: greetings, I/you/he/she, this, friend, teacher, student, name, nationality, where from, yes/is…? | Nationality (-ī / -iyya); question words ما · مَنْ · هَلْ · مِنْ أَيْنَ | Greet, introduce yourself, ask someone's name, nationality and where they're from | A1 |
| **02 The family** | 40: family members, to read, to live, to have, to pray, wuḍūʾ, numbers 3 and 7–10, how many, mosque, prayer words | **7 cards:** three case endings (-u/-a/-i); present tense "I/he" by its first letter; the past tense endings; counting 3–10; "we" verbs; why verbs come in pairs; عِنْدِي "I have" | Say who's in your family and how many; "I pray / he prays"; "I went" | A1 (grammar load: A1–A2) |
| **03 Housing** | 45: house, rooms, furniture, floors, days of the week, numbers 1, 2, 5, 6, first/third | "There is" (فِي الْغُرْفَةِ سَرِيرٌ); "this is a house" vs "this house"; numbers 3–10 work backwards; كَمْ + singular; ordinals after the noun; لَيْسَ "is not" | Describe your home; give a floor and a flat number; say what's there and what isn't | A1 |
| **04 Daily life** | 40: telling the time, morning/evening/night, meals, transport, clothes, yesterday | The present for habits; -an words for how/when (early, late); بِـ "by bus"; لا "don't" | Describe your day: when you get up, how you travel, what you don't do | A1 |
| **05 Food & drink** | 40: food, drink, meals, hungry, restaurant, to eat/prefer/come/like | Verb + object; "prefer X to Y"; لِأَنَّ "because"; hungry/thirsty forms | Say what you like and prefer and why; order simply | A1 |
| **06 Prayer** | 39: the prayer times, Masjid al-Ḥarām and the Prophet's Mosque, the Kaaba, ʿumrah words, Eid, zakāt al-fiṭr, "may God reward you" | Active participle "I'm going" (ذاهِبٌ); لِـ "for"; names that don't take -n (Makkah); next to, in front of | Talk about going to pray and the parts of ʿumrah; the phrases Muslims use every day | A1 (religious vocab: beyond A1) |
| **07 Study** | 40: study, school day, subjects, college, library, exam, books, pen | لِـ + verb "in order to"; كَمْ again; "they (men)" ـُونَ | Talk about your studies and timetable | A1+ |
| **08 Work** | 22: hours, professions and colleges, hospital, pharmacy, company | دَرَسَ vs دَرَّسَ (doubling changes meaning); words ending in ى; "how many hours?" | Say what you do and where you work | A1+ |
| **09 Shopping** | 40: prices, numbers 20–100, colours (both forms), clothes, groceries | "How much?" بِكَمْ؛ أُرِيدُ + -a; colours never take -n; numbers 11–99 + singular | Ask prices, say what you want and its colour | A1+ / A2 (numbers) |
| **10 Weather** | 40: seasons, hot/cold, sky, sea, temperature, tomorrow | كانَ "was" + -a; لَيْسَ again; لِذَلِكَ "so" | Talk about the weather now and yesterday | A2 (grammar) |
| **11 People & places** | 26: village/city, directions N/S/E/W, travel, crowding, pollution, opinion | لَكِنَّ "but" + -a; "there isn't"; feminine colours; the dual (ساعَتَيْنِ) | Compare a village and a city; give a simple opinion | A2 (grammar) |
| **12 Hobbies** | 26: sport, swimming, reading, calligraphy, collecting stamps, magazines | Plurals in ـات; لِأَنَّها; verbs ending in ى (أَهْوى) | Say what you like doing and why | A2 (grammar) |

**What the Practise area adds:**
- **Verbs:** the full 6-person tables (I, you m/f, he, she, we) in past, present and **future**, so the future does exist, but only here.
- **Joining words:** *if, when, before, after, sometimes, never, in my opinion*.
- **Essentials:** all the numbers, the days and months, and the Islamic months.

These are valuable, but they're optional and never come back in review.

## 4. What a daily learner knows, and when (10 minutes a day)

Assumptions:
- The learner does the daily goal of 2 steps every day, passes the unit tests first time, and keeps up their review.
- **"Realistic"** adds reviews counting toward the goal, test retries and a few missed days, which comes to roughly 1.3× longer.

| Day (ideal) | Realistic | Where they are | What they can actually do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | week 1 | Reading Arabic done | Read short vowelled words slowly. Recognise every letter in all its forms |
| 6–9 | week 2 | The basics done | Build "this is my house", "I'm a student", "the student's book". ~35 words |
| 10–14 | week 2–3 | Unit 01 | Greet and introduce themselves; ask name and nationality. ~75 words |
| 15–25 | weeks 3–5 | Units 02–03 | Family, home, numbers to 10, "I have", "there is", "is not". Unit 02 is the first wall: case endings, present **and** past in one unit. ~160 words |
| 26–40 | weeks 5–8 | Units 04–06 | Daily routine, food, the prayer and ʿumrah vocabulary. ~280 words |
| 41–68 | weeks 8–13 | Units 07–12 | Study, work, shopping, weather, places, hobbies; "was", "but", the dual, numbers to 100. ~445 path words |
| Any time | — | Your salah | Understand the daily prayer word by word: 224 different words across the prayer and 10 short surahs |

**Their level after about 3 months**, if they kept going:

| Skill | Level | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Reading vowelled Arabic | **A1+, some A2** on familiar topics | Every line in the app is fully vowelled; lots of reading and building sentences |
| Reading unvowelled Arabic (signs, news, WhatsApp) | **pre-A1** | Never practised: every word always shows its vowels |
| Listening | **A1**, only for slow, clear MSA on course topics | All audio is clean, slow MSA. Natural speech, khutbahs and any dialect will be out of reach |
| Speaking | **A1 at best** | Answers are typed or built from tiles. Speaking with the microphone is only in the Practise pages, and in browsers that support it |
| Writing | **A1** (keyboard) | Typed answers and tiles; no handwriting |
| Vocabulary | ~450 path words met, plus any of the ~220 practice items they open; **~350–450 really known** with steady review | FSRS keeps reviewed words at ~90% |
| Grammar (recognising it) | **Good A1, reaching A2**: case endings, numbers 3–10, كانَ / لَيْسَ, dual, sound plurals | But **no** future, past or future negation, or "who/which" on the path, all of which A2 needs |
| The salah | **The strongest result**: they know what they're saying in every part of the prayer and in 10 short surahs | It's built for exactly this, with al-Husary's recitation and word timings |

**Honest summary of the level:** "can read the Qurʾānic script slowly, understands the daily prayer, and has solid A1 Modern Standard Arabic foundations". Not "can hold a conversation", and not "understands the Qurʾān" beyond the salah and the short surahs.

## 5. Being realistic

- **Hours:**
  - The path is ~12½ hours of listed time, perhaps 20–25 hours with review.
  - **Cambridge:** roughly 90–100 guided hours to reach **A1** and 180–200 to **A2**, counted from beginner (measured for English).
  - **FSI:** Arabic takes about **88 weeks / 2,200 class hours** to professional level, against ~24–30 weeks for the easiest languages, so it's about 3× harder.
  - So finishing Rafiq ≈ having **covered** A1 (and some A2) material, but with far fewer practice hours than A1 usually needs. Retention and confidence come from **repetition**, which is why review matters more than adding more units.
- **MSA versus dialects:** Rafiq teaches Modern Standard Arabic, the language of the Qurʾān, prayer, khutbahs and news. Arabs speak dialects in daily life, so a learner won't follow everyday conversation in Cairo or Riyadh. That's the right choice for this audience, but say it plainly on the site.
- **Real usage today:**
  - About 85% of sign-ups don't come back.
  - On 19 Oct no learner had studied on more than 5 days.
  - So far, the content beyond unit 03 has hardly been tested on real learners. **The first week (units 00, 0b and 01) is where almost everyone is.**

## 6. Gaps and errors found (data)

**Errors and duplicates** (for Samir, in `vocab-data.js` / `path-data.js`):

| Problem | Where | Fix |

| "Water heater" tagged as Food & drink; "hostess, air stewardess" in the Food unit | word list | Retag; consider dropping |

**Rare words that take up slots:**
- College of Aviation / Nursing / Pharmacy / Engineering (5 college words in unit 08)
- Ruqʿa script, Naskh script, stamp collecting, correspondence
- Horsemanship, she-camel, tray of eggs, packet of salt, water heater
- Tenant, the *ifāḍa* and farewell ṭawāfs, the *mīqāt*

The ʿumrah words do suit Rafiq's audience. The others mostly come from the textbook's dialogues.

**Core words missing from the path** (checked across the path, the basics, the Practise area and the word list):
- **Verbs:** *know* (أَعْرِفُ), *understand* (أَفْهَمُ / لا أَفْهَمُ), *take*, *need*, *ask*, *answer*, *find*, *return* (as a word, not only inside sentences), *forget*, *remember*.
  - *Give, help, do, sit, enter* are in the word list but **not on the path**.
- **Phrases:** *hello* (مَرْحَبًا).
- **Nouns:** *word* (كَلِمَة), *question* (as a taught word), *answer*.
- **Adjectives:** *easy* is only in sentences; *bad* and *sad* are missing.

**Grammar missing from the path** (A1–A2 essentials):
- **Future:** سَـ / سَوْفَ, in the Verbs practise only.
- **Past negation:** لَمْ + verb (or ما + past).
- **Future negation:** لَنْ.
- **"Who / which":** الَّذِي / الَّتِي as a taught point.
- **"Must":** يَجِبُ أَنْ.
- **Things that appear in sentences but are never explained on a card:** comparatives (أَكْبَرُ مِنْ); commands (اُكْتُبْ، اِسْمَعْ); object endings on verbs (أُحِبُّكَ، يُساعِدُنِي).

**Shape problems:**
- **Unit 02 is overloaded:** 7 cards, including the case endings, the present tense, the **whole past tense** and numbers 3–10. That's the first big drop-off point for a beginner.
- **Uneven units:** units 08, 11 and 12 are thin (22–26 words); unit 03 has 45.
- **Practice the path never uses:** verbs, joining words and essentials sit outside the path, so they **never come back in review**, and the future tense only exists there.
- **One-way skills:** no listening at natural speed, and no reading without vowels. Speaking on the path is typed.

## 7. Improving the two starter units (00 Reading Arabic and 0b The basics)

These matter most: they're the first week, and nearly every learner sees them.

### Reading Arabic (unit 00)

1. **Add the missing spellings as 5 more short reading rules**, each with a tap-to-hear example and a quick check, like the 3 already there:
   - **Hamza and its seats:** أ إ ؤ ئ ء (أَنا، سُؤال، مَساء).
   - **Madda:** آ (آمِين، القُرْآن).
   - **Alif maqṣūra:** ى (عَلى، مُصْطَفى).
   - **The dagger alif:** ٰ (هٰذا، الرَّحْمٰنِ، ذٰلِكَ), which is in **line one of al-Fātiḥah**.
   - **The joining alif (hamzat al-waṣl)** and lām-alif لا: بِسْمِ اللهِ is "bismillāh", not "bi-ismi".

   The code comment for the rules step already promises "sun letters, the joining alif", but only 3 rules exist.
2. **End with a payoff: "Read your first line".** The learner reads بِسْمِ اللهِ الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ and الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعالَمِينَ word by word (tap to check each against al-Husary). This links the starter to the reason most people sign up.
3. **"Read it, then hear it" practice:** 15–20 vowelled words, from easy (بابٌ) to hard (يَتَوَضَّأُونَ). The learner says each one, or picks how it's said, before hearing it.
4. **Bring the letters back in review for the first two weeks.** Today `reached()` leaves the reading starter out of review, so a learner who struggled with ح/ه/خ never sees them again. Two or three letter-and-sound items per review session would fix that, and stop once they're steady.
5. **Move the sun and moon letters into the reading rules.** They're about reading; the basics can then just recall them.
6. **Say how long it really takes:** "about an hour, over a few days". The starter is ~51 listed minutes, but most beginners need 2–3 short sessions per letter group.

### The basics (unit 0b)

1. **Spread it out.** It packs ال and sun letters, the possessive endings, ة → ت, the pronouns, "no is", gender, هَذا/هَذِهِ, iḍāfa, adjective agreement, broken plurals and the plural pronouns into ~36 minutes. Two options:
   - Split "Masculine and feminine" and "'Of', and describing words" into two lessons each.
   - Add a **"put it together"** lesson before the check: build 6 sentences of the goal type, for example "this is my big house", "the teacher's book is new".
2. **Add a "survival Arabic" lesson**, either the last basics lesson or the first step of unit 01. These are the phrases that let a learner keep going in a class, a masjid or a chat:
   - مَرْحَبًا hello
   - لا أَفْهَمُ I don't understand
   - مَرَّةً أُخْرى، مِنْ فَضْلِكَ again, please
   - بِبُطْءٍ، مِنْ فَضْلِكَ slowly, please
   - ما مَعْنى …؟ what does … mean?
   - كَيْفَ نَقُولُ … بِالْعَرَبِيَّةِ؟ how do you say … in Arabic?
   - أَعْرِفُ / لا أَعْرِفُ I know / I don't know

   A teacher should check the wording, and the lines need audio.
3. **Preview the first verbs** with one lesson, "I go, I study, I pray: the أَ at the start means I" (present tense, first person only). Unit 02 currently brings the present, the past and the case endings all at once. A small preview in the basics makes that first wall lower.
4. **Show the goal sentence at the start of the basics as well as at the end**, so the learner knows where the 36 minutes are heading.

## 8. Improving the rest (in order)

1. **Fix the data errors** in section 6 (kidney, the 4 × ساعة, the double week and night). Small, safe, Samir's files.
2. **Rebalance unit 02:** move "the past: the ending shows who" to unit 04 or 05, or to the planned "Telling stories" unit, and the case-endings card to unit 03. Keep 02 about family, numbers and "I have".
3. **Swap ~20 rare words for core ones** (list in section 6): fewer faculties, scripts and stamps; more *know, understand, take, give, need, help, ask, answer, return, word, question, easy, good/bad, hello*.
4. **Add the missing A1–A2 grammar cards** where they fit:
   - the future (سَـ) in 04 "Daily life" or 10 "The weather" ("tomorrow it will rain");
   - لَمْ in 10, next to كانَ;
   - الَّذِي / الَّتِي in 11;
   - comparatives (أَكْبَرُ مِنْ) in 11 "Village and city";
   - commands in 05 (ordering) or 07 (classroom).
5. **Make the Practise area count:** put verb tables and joining words into review once the path has taught the verb or the word.
6. **Speaking on the path:** offer the microphone on "Say it yourself" where the browser supports it (Drills, Verbs and Joining words already do this), with typing as the fallback. Add a **listen and repeat (shadowing)** step to every unit's conversation.
7. **Listening and reading for real life:**
   - a **natural-speed** replay of each unit's conversation;
   - after unit 06, an optional **"hide the vowels"** switch for review, so learners start reading Arabic as it's usually printed.
8. **Show progress as can-do statements:**
   - Every 3–4 units, a short **level check** ("You can now introduce yourself and your family: A1").
   - Show the **known-words count**, which already exists as `masteredWords()`, with milestones at 100, 250 and 500 words.
9. **Next units:** the four "coming" units (Travel, Health, Ramadan & Eid, Telling stories) are the right A2 step. "Telling stories" is the natural home for the past tense and لَمْ. A **salah and short surahs reading track** (read the line, then hear al-Husary) would build on Rafiq's strongest result.

## 9. Other things worth knowing

- **Licence:** the topic order (and much of the vocabulary) follows *Al-ʿArabiyyah Bayna Yadayk* Book 1. The repo already notes that the book is Arabic For All's copyright and that Rafiq is an unofficial study aid (PROMOS.md). Keep the dialogues, sentences and explanations Rafiq's own, which they are (`src: "rafiq"`), and avoid "follows the book word by word" in marketing. Replacing the rare textbook words (section 6) also makes the course more clearly Rafiq's.
- **Honest claims for the site and videos:**
  - **Safe:**
    - "Read Arabic letters in about a week."
    - "Understand every word of your salah."
    - "Strong beginner (A1) foundations in about 3 months at 10 minutes a day."
  - **Avoid:** "fluent", "speak Arabic", "understand the Qurʾān". Only the salah and the short surahs are covered.
- **What to measure:** with real usage at 1–5 days, the most useful numbers are how many finish **the reading starter**, **the basics** and **unit 01**, and the known-words count at day 7 and day 30. These say more than unit 12 completion, which no one has reached yet.
- **Quality checks that already exist:** `tools/camel-check.py` (Arabic in the word list) and `tools/typesafe-exp/qa_dialogues.py` (grammar of the dialogue lines). Rerun both after any of the word swaps above.
