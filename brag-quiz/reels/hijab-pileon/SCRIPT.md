**[The pile-on: "We need to BAN the hijab!" … hijab… hijab… HIJAB! HIJAB! — dead cut]**

**[Walks into frame.]** Hijab.
Everybody seems to have an opinion on it… but what does the word actually mean?

**The root.** Hijab comes from three letters: ḥā, jīm, bā (ح ج ب) 🎬 *the three letters drop in*. Ḥajaba means to screen something off, to block it from view.
From the same root you get *ḥājib*: the doorkeeper, the one who stands at the door and screens who comes in. And *ḥājib* is also the Arabic for your **eyebrow** 🎬 *an eyebrow over a closed eye*, because it shields the eye.

**In the Qur'an.** Every time the word *ḥijāb* comes up in the Qur'an, it means a screen, a barrier 🎬 *quick ayah cards*:
- between the people of Paradise and the people of the Fire, there's a *ḥijāb* (al-Aʿrāf 7:46)
- the sun goes down and hides behind the *ḥijāb* (Ṣād 38:32)
- Maryam goes off and puts a *ḥijāb* between herself and her people (Maryam 19:17)
- and Allah spoke to Mūsā *min warāʾi ḥijāb*, "from behind a ḥijāb" (ash-Shūrā 42:51)

The famous one is al-Aḥzāb 33:53: when you ask the Prophet's wives ﷺ for something, ask them from behind a *ḥijāb*, a screen 🎬 *a curtain*. Anas, who was there, said it came down at the wedding of Zaynab bint Jaḥsh (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4791).

**So where's the headscarf?** Allah uses other words for the clothing 🎬 *two word cards*:
- *khimār*: "let them draw their *khumur* over their chests" (an-Nūr 24:31). ʿĀʾishah said that when this came down, the women of the Muhājirīn tore their cloaks and covered themselves with them, straight away (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 4758).
- *jilbāb*: the outer garment (al-Aḥzāb 33:59).
And look at the verse right before 24:31: it's for the **men**: "tell the believing men to lower their gaze" (an-Nūr 24:30). The screen starts with the eyes.

**What it really means.** So *hijab* isn't just a piece of cloth. It's a screen that protects something precious, the way your eyebrow protects your eye. It goes hand in hand with *ḥayāʾ*, modesty, and the Prophet ﷺ said: "Ḥayāʾ is part of faith" (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 24). Even Allah's own veil, he said, is light (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 179) 🎬 *light*.

**Close.** So next time everyone has an opinion on the hijab… now you know what the word actually means.
