# Aḥad: Bilāl's one word (#315)

Owner, 1 Nov 2026: the word أَحَد and Bilāl's "Aḥad, aḥad!". Talking to camera; my graphics go on top (🎬).

**What to say carefully.**
- Bilāl's torture and "aḥad, aḥad" are in **the sīrah** (Ibn Isḥāq, in Sīrat Ibn Hishām) and in **Ibn Mājah 150** (Ibn
  Masʿūd: the first seven to show their Islam; Bilāl "made light of himself for Allah… and he kept saying: aḥad, aḥad").
  So we say "the sīrah tells us" / "it's narrated", not "in Bukhari".
- **aḥad vs wāḥid**: both come from و ح د (one). wāḥid is the number one: it can be first of many, and can be part of
  something. aḥad, said of Allah, is the One with no second, nothing like Him, not made of parts. In a negative sentence
  aḥad means "no one" (al-Ikhlāṣ ends with it: وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ). Classical scholars (e.g. al-Azharī)
  note that aḥad as a description in a positive sentence is used for Allah alone. That's the depth: one word that says
  both "Allah is One" and "no one is equal to Him", said under the man who claimed to own him.

## Version A: the full one (about 80 s) ← recommended (TypeSafe: finish 2.75, share 2.71, fair 2.61 · B: 2.77 / 2.67 / 2.42)

> **Under a burning rock, he could only say one word. And that one word is a whole creed.** 🎬 *black screen, a single
> word fades up: أَحَد*
>
> Makkah. The middle of the day, the sand so hot it burns. A slave from Abyssinia, **Bilāl**, is dragged out by his master,
> **Umayyah**. 🎬 *drawn: the white-hot sun, the sand, a faceless figure on the ground* They lay him on the sand and put
> a huge rock on his chest. *"Deny Muhammad, and worship al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā."*
>
> And Bilāl says one word. **"Aḥad. Aḥad."** 🎬 *أَحَد slams on, twice, with a shake*
>
> Why that word? Why not **wāḥid**, the normal word for one? 🎬 *وَاحِد ← و ح د → أَحَد*
>
> Because **wāḥid** is a number. One… then two, then three. Something that's one can still be one *of* many, or made of
> parts. 🎬 *1, 2, 3 counting up next to وَاحِد*
>
> **Aḥad** is different. It's the One with **no second**. Nothing like Him, nothing next to Him, nothing He's made of. It's
> the word Allah uses for Himself: **قُلْ هُوَ اللّٰهُ أَحَدٌ**, *"Say: He is Allah, the One."* 🎬 *ayah card, al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1*
>
> And here's the beautiful part. The same surah ends with the same word: **وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ**, *"and there
> is **no one** equal to Him."* 🎬 *112:4, with أَحَدٌ lit up at the start and the end* In a negative sentence, aḥad means
> **no one**.
>
> So when Bilāl says *aḥad*, he's saying two things at once. **Allah is One.** And **no one** is equal to Him: not al-Lāt,
> not al-ʿUzzā, and not the man standing over him who thinks he owns him. 🎬 *the idols and Umayyah's shadow crack and fade*
>
> A man with a rock on his chest, and he was the free one.
>
> Abū Bakr bought him and set him free. 🎬 *the rock lifts; light* And years later, when Makkah was opened, it was Bilāl
> who climbed onto the Kaʿbah and gave the adhān. 🎬 *the Kaʿbah, a figure on top, the adhān's first words*
>
> The Prophet ﷺ even told him: *"I heard your footsteps ahead of me in Jannah."* 🎬 *al-Bukhārī 1149*
>
> We say *aḥad* every time we recite al-Ikhlāṣ. Next time, say it like Bilāl did. 🤍
>
> If you could only say one word to show what you believe, what would it be? Comment it below. 👇

## Version B: tighter (about 50 s)

> **Bilāl could only say one word, and it's the deepest word in Arabic.** 🎬 *أَحَد*
>
> Makkah, midday. Bilāl on the burning sand, a rock on his chest. "Deny Muhammad." And he says: **"Aḥad. Aḥad."**
>
> Not *wāḥid*, the number one. Because one can be one of many. **Aḥad** is the One with no second, the word Allah uses
> for Himself: *qul huwa Allāhu aḥad* (al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1).
>
> And at the end of the same surah: *wa lam yakun lahu kufuwan aḥad*. *No one* is equal to Him (112:4). Aḥad means
> both.
>
> So with one word, Bilāl said: Allah is One, and **no one**, not the idols, not the man standing over him, is equal to
> Him. With a rock on his chest, he was the free one.
>
> Years later, he gave the adhān from on top of the Kaʿbah.
>
> Next time you recite al-Ikhlāṣ, say *aḥad* like Bilāl did. 🤍 If you could only say one word to show what you believe,
> what would it be? 👇
