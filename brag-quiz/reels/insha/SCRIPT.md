# In shāʾ Allāh: not a polite no (#314)

Owner, 1 Nov 2026: go deeper than "we say it as a polite no". It's tawakkul: handing yourself to Allah, free from anxiety
about the future, because it only happens if Allah has willed it. Talking to camera; my graphics go on top (🎬).

**A note on the past tense.** After إِنْ ("if"), the past form شاءَ points to the future: grammatically it means "if Allah
wills". So we don't say the grammar *means* "already decided" (IDEAS.md flags that mistake on m1). We say the word *is
in the past form*, and that this fits what the Prophet ﷺ taught: everything is already written. That's true and still
lands the point.

## Version A: the full one (about 85 s) ← recommended (TypeSafe: finish 2.73, share 2.74, fair 2.44 · B: 2.74 / 2.73 / 2.35)

> **We've turned in shāʾ Allāh into a polite no. It's the opposite.** 🎬 *"IN SHĀʾ ALLĀH = NO?" with the "NO" struck through*
>
> "Are you coming tonight?" "In shāʾ Allāh…" And we all know what that means. 😅 🎬 *two chat bubbles; the second fades
> to grey: "= no"* But it's actually one of the most freeing things you can ever say.
>
> It's three words. **In**: if. **Shāʾa**: willed. **Allāh**. 🎬 *إِنْ · شاءَ · اللّٰهُ split apart, each with its meaning*
>
> And look at that verb: **shāʾa**. It's in the **past** tense. 🎬 *شاءَ, "PAST" stamped on it* After "if" it points to
> the future, but the word itself is in the past, and that fits what the Prophet ﷺ taught: **"The pens have been lifted,
> and the pages have dried."** 🎬 *hadith card: رُفِعَتِ الأَقْلامُ وَجَفَّتِ الصُّحُفُ · at-Tirmidhī 2516*
>
> Your future isn't being decided as you panic about it. It's already written.
>
> Even the root tells you this. Shāʾa comes from the same root as **shayʾ**, *a thing*. 🎬 *ش ي ء → شاءَ · شَيْء* In
> Arabic, a "thing" is literally **something willed**.
>
> That's why Allah says in Surah al-Kahf: **"Never say of anything, 'I'll do that tomorrow', except: if Allah wills."**
> 🎬 *ayah card 18:23–24; لِشَيْءٍ and يَشاءَ light up together* The *thing*, and the *will*, side by side, from the same
> root.
>
> So in shāʾ Allāh isn't "no". It means: **I'll do my part, and the result is with Allah.** The Prophet ﷺ said: **tie
> your camel, then put your trust in Allah.** 🎬 *a drawn camel, a rope tied, then light; at-Tirmidhī 2517*
>
> That's **tawakkul**. It comes from **wakīl**: the one you hand your affairs over to. 🎬 *تَوَكُّل ← و ك ل → وَكِيل*
>
> And that's where the freedom is. The job, the exam, the marriage, the results… 🎬 *worries stack up as he names them*
> If it's written for you, nothing can stop it. If it isn't, nothing can bring it. **"If the whole world gathered to
> benefit you with something, they couldn't, except with what Allah had already written for you."** 🎬 *at-Tirmidhī
> 2516; the stack of worries falls away*
>
> Allah even tells us *why* He told us it's written: **"so that you don't despair over what you missed, nor gloat over
> what you were given."** 🎬 *ayah card, al-Ḥadīd 57:23*
>
> So next time you say in shāʾ Allāh, don't say it as a way out. **Say it as letting go.** You plan, you work, and you hand
> the rest to the One who already knows how it ends. 🎬 *اللّٰه, slow push-in*
>
> What's one thing you're worried about right now? **Write it in the comments with in shāʾ Allāh**, and let's make duʿāʾ
> for each other. 🤲

## Version B: tighter (about 55 s)

> We've turned **in shāʾ Allāh** into a polite no. But it's the opposite. It's the most freeing thing you can say.
>
> **In**: if. **Shāʾa**: willed. **Allāh**. And notice: shāʾa is in the **past** tense. Fitting, because the Prophet ﷺ
> said, **"The pens have been lifted, and the pages have dried."** (at-Tirmidhī 2516) It's already written.
>
> Allah says: **"Never say of anything, 'I'll do that tomorrow', except: if Allah wills."** (al-Kahf 18:23–24) And
> *anything* there is **shayʾ**: from the same root as *shāʾa*. In Arabic, a thing is literally *something willed*.
>
> So in shāʾ Allāh means: **I tie my camel, and I trust Allah with the rest.** That's tawakkul.
>
> The job, the exam, the marriage. If it's written for you, nothing can stop it. If it isn't, nothing can bring it. So
> stop carrying tomorrow on your shoulders. **Say it, and let it go.**
